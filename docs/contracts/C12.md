# C12. The control door (agent-browser's service <-> the agentwatch daemon)

**The master of C12 (board task ab-v16.94.1).** This file is the master for C12. C12 is new in board task
ab-v16.94.1, stage 0 of the self-contained agent browser. It binds this repository (the service, `agent-browser
service`) and agentwatch (its daemon), and no other. Its source is the design
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-self-contained-agent-browser-design.md`, section 7.1: the daemon lead's
proposal and review (R1, R7, m1, C4, C7 and C8), the operator's answers of 2026-11-10 (its section 19), and the
daemon lead's answers to stage 0's choices (2026-11-10). In this file C1 to C10 are the contracts of
chrome-extension `docs/protocol.md`, and C11 is `docs/contracts/C11.md`. The vectors: `test/vectors/control.json`.

| Part | Side | From |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The door, its admission, the hello, the service's answers and its events | agent-browser's service | stage 1 (boards ab-v16.94.2 to ab-v16.94.5), off by default |
| The client, its check of the service, the provenance and the requests | the agentwatch daemon | stage 2, off by default |

Nothing uses C12 before stage 1. The old daemon path stays the default, and it does not change, until stage 3
turns the switch on for a box (with the operator's approval). The switch moves the parties of C4 and C10 from
the daemon to the service on that box; C12 is how the daemon then talks to the service.

**The door.**

- A Unix socket with mode 0600, `control.sock`, in the service's run folder (mode 0700):
  `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/agent-browser/` on Linux, `~/.cache/agent-browser/run/` on the Mac. A path is at most 103
  bytes. The door has no TCP port.
- One JSON object per line, in compact UTF-8 with a newline at its end: no ASCII escapes of characters that
  UTF-8 can hold (with them a valid C2 payload can grow to three times its size). A line is at most
  `CONTROL_MAX_LINE = 33619968` bytes (the 33,554,432 bytes of a C2 payload and 64 KiB more).
- The daemon measures each line before it sends it. When a line is longer than `CONTROL_MAX_LINE`, the daemon
  does not send it, and it answers its own caller with a 413. So the service never has to close the control
  connection for a line's size. A side that still reads a longer line closes the connection.
- The daemon sends the requests. The service sends the answers and the events. The service sends no request.
- No side logs page content, a cookie, a URL path, a payload, a token, a token's fingerprint, a username, a password, a
  passphrase, a wrapped key, a sealed record, a pane's seal or a form value in any log line. A line that a side refuses
  for its size or its shape is never logged.

**Who connects: only the agentwatch daemon process itself (the daemon lead's R1).** At each accept the service
reads the peer (`SO_PEERCRED` and `SO_PEERPIDFD` on Linux) and admits it only when all of these hold:

1. The service gets the MainPID of agentwatch's unit from the user's systemd at this accept, and it holds a
   pidfd of the MainPID itself. A daemon restart changes the MainPID.
2. The peer is the MainPID itself, or a direct child of it: the peer's parent pid is the MainPID, and the
   parent's start time (field 22 of `/proc/<ppid>/stat`) equals the MainPID's start time, read in the same
   check. (If agentwatch's unit makes the daemon itself its MainPID, the child case goes, and this file changes
   first.)
3. The peer is the daemon's command. The service reads the expected command from a file that agentwatch's
   daemon writes at its start, before it connects, so an agentwatch update needs no restart of the service. It compares the peer's command line
   (`/proc/<pid>/cmdline`) from argv[1] on: argv[0] is the interpreter's path in uv's cache, which changes with
   the script's dependencies (agentwatch runs a uv script). It also checks the peer's executable
   (`/proc/<pid>/exe`) against the interpreter that the file names. The part "The daemon's expected command",
   below, names the file's path and its format.
4. The peer is the same process during the whole check: the service holds its pidfd, and the peer's start time
   is the same before and after the reads, and not earlier than its parent's (start times count in clock ticks,
   so a child can start in its parent's tick). Its parent pid is the same before and after the reads. After the
   checks the service reads the MainPID again, and it must be the same.

- A process of agentwatch's cgroup is not enough. A child that the daemon forks and that connects itself has
  the daemon, not the MainPID, as its parent, and it is refused. The daemon never passes its connection to
  another process.
- The service's own pid never passes. No agent-browser CLI command is a control client.
- The service checks at most 4 connections at a time. A connection over that limit gets the refused line with
  its reason (`The control door checks 4 other connections now, so it refuses this one: connect again.`), and
  the service closes it.
- A peer that fails a rule of 1 to 4 gets one line `{"t":"refused","reason":"<text>"}` that names the rule, and
  the service closes the connection and logs the reason.
- When the check cannot run, the door refuses (the daemon lead's m1). If the systemd query fails, or the daemon
  runs with no systemd user unit, the service sends the same line with the reason, closes the connection, and
  logs the reason. The daemon shows that line in `agentwatch health`. The service never admits by a weaker
  rule.
- The daemon checks the service too. Before it sends its hello, the daemon admits the peer of `control.sock`
  only when that peer is the MainPID of `agent-browser.service`, by a pidfd and its start time. Else it closes
  the connection and sends nothing: a process of the same user that replaced `control.sock` would otherwise
  get sign-in payloads and list acts.
- On the Mac the service reads `LOCAL_PEERPID`, gets the job's pid from `launchctl print gui/<uid>/<agentwatch's label>`
  (its `pid` line) at each accept, reads the parent and the start time with `proc_pidinfo` and the command line
  with `sysctl` (`KERN_PROCARGS2`), and applies the same rules (stage 4; not run on the Mac yet).

**The daemon's expected command (rule 3's file; board ab-v16.94.2).** agentwatch's daemon writes this file at its
start, before it connects. The service reads it at each accept, so a new file takes effect at the next
connection, with no restart of the service.

- The path: `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/agent-browser/daemon-command.json`, or `~/.config/agent-browser/daemon-command.json`
  when `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` is not set, on Linux and on the Mac. agentwatch's unit passes `--daemon-command <file>`
  and `--daemon-unit <unit>` explicitly (the command, below): a systemd user unit's environment can lack the
  user's `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`.
- The format: one JSON object in UTF-8, `{"v":1,"interpreter":"<path>","args":["<argument>",...]}`. `v` is 1.
  `interpreter` is the absolute, resolved path (`realpath`) of the daemon's interpreter, because `/proc/<pid>/exe`
  resolves links: a path through a link never matches. `args` is the daemon's command line from argv[1] on, one
  string for each argument, in order. The daemon takes `args` from `/proc/self/cmdline`, not from Python's
  `sys.argv` (which drops the interpreter's flags; `-m` changes argv[0]). The service ignores a key that it does
  not know.
- The check: the target of the peer's `/proc/<pid>/exe` equals `interpreter`, and the peer's
  `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` from argv[1] on equals `args`, with the same count and each argument the same. When the
  interpreter is replaced on disk, `/proc/<pid>/exe` ends with ` (deleted)`: the door then refuses the daemon
  (rule 3) until the daemon restarts and writes the file again.
- The file is a regular file, not a link, owned by the service's user, and not writable by its group or by
  others (agentwatch writes it with mode 0600). The service opens it once, with no link followed (`O_NOFOLLOW`),
  and checks the open file (`fstat`), so the file that it checks is the file that it reads. A file that is missing, that breaks one of these rules, or whose
  content breaks the format fails rule 3 at each connection: the refused line names rule 3 and the reason.
- An example, for a daemon that uv starts as `<python> /opt/agentwatch/bin/agentwatch --daemon --config
  /opt/agentwatch/config.yaml`:
  `{"v":1,"interpreter":"/opt/python/bin/python3.12","args":["/opt/agentwatch/bin/agentwatch","--daemon","--config","/opt/agentwatch/config.yaml"]}`.

**The hello (the daemon lead's C7).** After the admission each side sends its hello as its first line:

- The service: `{"t":"hello","v":1,"side":"service","version":"<agent-browser version>","commit":"<commit or null>","features":[...]}`.
- The daemon: `{"t":"hello","v":1,"side":"daemon","version":"<agentwatch version>","features":[...]}`.

- `v` is the version of this door's lines: 1. `version` is the side's package version. The service's
  `commit` is the 40-hex commit of agent-browser that it runs, or `null` when it cannot tell; the daemon
  compares it with its pin (the design, section 7.3). The `commit` is for the pin check only, never for trust.
- The daemon refuses a hello whose `side` is not `"service"`, and the service refuses a hello whose `side` is
  not `"daemon"`: each closes the connection.
- A side that reads a hello whose `v` is not 1 closes the connection and sends no request and no answer; the
  daemon shows it in `agentwatch health`. A new `v` is a new door, not an additive change.
- `features` lists the side's additive features. Fields and features are only added, never changed: a side
  ignores a field and a feature that it does not know. A request for an op that the service does not have
  gets `{"t":"res","id":"<id>","ok":false,"error":"unsupported"}`. So an older service and a newer daemon (or
  the reverse) degrade, with no lock-step bump.
- Feature names: `fake` (the hello of `agent-browser service --fake`, a scripted state with no Chrome, for
  agentwatch's hermetic tests), `mcp_token` (the service checks each MCP request's connection token against the
  `mcp_token` of `set_sessions`: the connection tokens, below), and `sign_ins` (the stored sign-ins, from stage 1e part
  1: their eight ops, their two events and the key `sign_ins` of `status`). A stage that adds an op or a field adds its
  feature name to this list first.
- `agent-browser service --hello` prints the service's hello as one line and exits, with no service running.
- The daemon sends no request before it has read the service's hello, and the service answers no request
  before it has read the daemon's hello.

**The lines.**

- A request: `{"t":"req","id":"<id>","op":"<op>","by":{"kind":"<kind>","user":"<user>","via":"<via>"},...}`,
  with the op's fields at the top level, as in C4. `id` is a string, unique on its connection.
- An answer: `{"t":"res","id":"<id>","ok":true,"data":{...}}` or `{"t":"res","id":"<id>","ok":false,"error":"<text>"}`.
  The service answers each request once.
- An event: `{"t":"ev","ev":"<event>",...}`, with the event's fields at the top level.
- No op field uses a line key: `t`, `v`, `id`, `op` or `by`.
- A line that is not a JSON object, or a request with no string `id`, closes the connection.
- A line is strict UTF-8, with no byte order mark and no CR: a line that is not valid UTF-8 (for example the bytes
  `ED A0 80` of a lone surrogate), a line that starts with a byte order mark (`EF BB BF`), and a line that holds a
  CR (byte `0D`, for example a line that ends in CR LF) break the compact-line rule, and each closes the
  connection.
- Bytes after the last newline are not a line: a side does not answer them. At the end of the stream it drops
  them and closes the connection. Many lines can come in one read: a side takes each of them, in order.
- A request with an op that the service does not have gets `unsupported` (the hello, above).
- A request whose `by` breaks the shape of provenance (below), or whose fields break the field rules (below),
  gets `{"t":"res","id":"<id>","ok":false,"error":"bad request"}`.
- A side ignores a line whose `t` it does not know: a newer side can add a kind of line. The daemon ignores an
  event whose `ev` it does not know, as it ignores a field that it does not know: a newer service can add an
  event.

**The field rules (the shape step).** The service checks each field of a request with these rules, and with no
other rule, before it checks the kind:

- A request carries each field of its row of the requests (for `config`, the four fields that it names). A
  request that omits one of them gets `bad request`. The shape step checks no field outside its row: a side
  ignores a field that it does not know, and `config` passes agentwatch's other `browser.*` keys as data.
- `scope` and `list` at the top level of a request, and `sessions[].scope`, match C11's scope pattern,
  `^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]{0,63}$`. `remote[].scope` and `remote[].list` are strings, and the shape step
  checks no pattern for them: `remote` stays empty until stage 5, which gives a remote scope a `<box>/` part
  (the self-contained design, section 9) and changes this rule first.
- A root (`roots` of `set_sessions`) follows C11's root rule: an object with an integer `pid` from 2 to
  2^53 - 1 and an integer `start` from 0 to 2^53 - 1. One root that breaks it makes the whole request a bad
  request (C11's line drops such an item; a snapshot of the daemon is never applied in part).
- `sessions` is a list of objects with a `scope`, a `repo` (a string or `null`), `roots` (a list of roots), a
  `cgroup` (a string or `null`), a `lifetime_id` (a string or `null`) and an `mcp_token`. `remote` is a list of
  objects with the strings `scope`, `box` and `list` and an `mcp_token`. `box` is an object with the lists of strings
  `hosts` and `cookie_names`.
- `mcp_token` (of an item of `sessions` or of `remote`) is `null` or 64 lowercase hex digits: the fingerprint of the
  session's connection token, the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the token's ASCII bytes (the connection tokens, below). It
  is never the token itself.
- One fingerprint appears at most once in a snapshot (in `sessions` and `remote` together). One scope appears at most
  once in `sessions`, and one remote scope at most once in `remote` (stage 5): the daemon sends no item for a launch
  name that two live sessions have, so neither of them gets a list or a token. The daemon sends `session_ended` for a
  launch name only when the last live session of that name ends: when one of two live sessions of a name ends, its next
  snapshot names the survivor with the survivor's own `lifetime_id` and an `mcp_token` that is the fingerprint of the
  token that the daemon minted at the survivor's own launch, or `null` when it minted none then (a session launched
  while the name was shared has none, so agentwatch writes no MCP entry for it: its client sends no `Authorization`
  header and gets the first 401 of C11's step 7, and only a request with a bearer gets the second 401). An item with `"lifetime_id":null` has
  `"mcp_token":null`. A snapshot that breaks one of these rules gets `bad request`, and the service keeps its old
  snapshot.
- `enabled` and `control` are booleans; `chrome_bin` is a string or `null`; `chrome_args` is a list of strings.
- `add` and `remove` are lists of strings, and each entry is checked later by the host rules (`list_set`,
  below). `prompt` and `host` are strings of one character or more.
- `payload` is an object. The shape step checks no more of it: the C2 check comes after the kind check, so an
  `import` with a token's kind gets `not the operator`, whatever its payload.
- `sealed` (the pane's seal) is an object with the strings `text`, `sender`, `rid` and `at`. `record` is an object
  with the strings `id`, `name`, `origin` and `path` and the list of strings `send_to`. `offer` is a boolean. The shape
  step checks no more of them: the rules of the stored sign-ins (below) come after the kind check.
- `key` is `null` or an object, and `records` is a list of objects. The shape step checks no more of them: a key or a
  record that breaks the rules of the stored sign-ins breaks only itself (below).
- `credential` is a string of `c-` and 8 lowercase hex digits: a credential's id, as in a record. Its name is not `id`,
  because `id` is a line key.

**Provenance (the daemon lead's R7).** Each request carries one shape of provenance:
`"by":{"kind":"operator"|"token"|"daemon","user":"<user>","via":"web"|"send"|"cli"|"config"|"daemon"}`.

- `kind: operator` only when the gateway's field is present with `"login":"password"`: the gateway adds
  `"operator":{"user":...,"login":"password"}` to the daemon's op only after its password-login check. The daemon
  refuses a list change, an allow, a dismissal, an import or an operator act of the stored sign-ins without it. A
  caller with the API token (an agent, or `agentwatch browser ...` in a terminal) never produces it. Stage 2 tests
  that an `operator` key in an HTTP body never gives `kind: operator`.
- `kind: token` for a start, a stop or a read with the API token (for a start and a stop, today's rule), and for an
  agent's offer of a stored sign-in (`sign_in_seal` with `"offer":true`; the stored sign-ins, below).
- `kind: daemon` for the daemon's own requests: `config`, `set_sessions`, `session_ended`, `launch_check`, `sign_ins`,
  the uninstall's `sign_ins_lock`, and the daemon's own reads. `browser.control` is a key of agentwatch's config file,
  so it comes in `config` with `kind: daemon` and `via: config`. The daemon's other own requests have `via: daemon`.
- `user` is the agentwatch user: the login's user for `operator`, the token's user for `token`, and the
  daemon's own user for `daemon`. It matches agentwatch's user pattern, `^[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_.-]{0,63}$`; a
  `user` that does not match breaks the shape of provenance (so no log line gets a newline or a control
  character from it).
- `via` is where the act came from: `web` (the web viewer), `send` (the operator extension's send, C9), `cli`
  (the agentwatch CLI), `config` (the config file), `daemon` (the daemon itself).
- The service checks a request in this order: its op (`unsupported`); the shape of its `by` and its fields
  (`bad request`); then its kind.
- The service applies an operator act (`list_set`, `prompt_allow`, `prompt_dismiss`, `import`, `sign_ins_setup`,
  `sign_ins_unlock`, `sign_ins_rekey`, `sign_in_reseal`, `sign_in_start_again`, and `sign_in_seal` with
  `"offer":false`) only with `kind: operator`; any other kind gets
  `{"t":"res","id":"<id>","ok":false,"error":"not the operator"}`.
- It applies `start` and `stop` only with `kind: operator` or `kind: token`, `sign_ins_lock` only with
  `kind: operator` or `kind: daemon`, `sign_in_seal` with `"offer":true` only with `kind: token`, and the daemon's own
  requests (`config`, `set_sessions`, `session_ended`, `launch_check`, `sign_ins`) only with `kind: daemon`; another
  kind gets `bad request`. The reads (`status`, `lists_get`, `prompts_get`, `endpoint`, `sender`) take any kind.
- The service does not check `via`: it logs it.
- The service takes provenance only from the control door, never from a client request (C11), and it logs
  each act with its provenance.

Stage 1a (board ab-v16.94.2) fixes the keys of the `data` of `status`, `lists_get`, `prompts_get`, `endpoint` and
`sender` in the part "The data of the reads", below, with the daemon lead's review. They include:

- the agent's text and the operator's text of a state as two separate keys, because the gateway gives the
  operator's text only to a password login;
- each prompt's recipients, the session name and the lifetime id of each session that the block stopped,
  because the daemon sends them the allow notice (today's gateway does, at an allow);
- the MCP's state, for `agentwatch health`.

The design's table names the prompt's field `id`; here it is `prompt`, because `id` is the request's id. The
design's table names a field `providers` for `list_set` too. No list has providers: the operator chose the
allow prompt, not a list of sign-in providers (board item ab-pbk). So `list_set` has no such field, and a
later rule can add it as an additive field. The design's `list_set` names the whole list (`allow`); here it adds
and removes (the daemon lead's change of 2026-11-10).

**The data of the reads (stage 1a).** The `data` of each read has these keys:

| Read | Its answer's `data` |
| --- | --- |
| `status` | `{"state","agent_text","operator_text","chrome_kind","chrome_version","chrome_pid","extension_version","update","endpoint","mcp","sessions","lists","last_stop","prompts","sign_ins","idle_stop_minutes"}` |
| `lists_get` | `{"lists":[{"name","repo","allow","sessions","error"}],"old_allow"}` |
| `prompts_get` | `{"prompts":[{"id","host","list","repo","scopes","from","warnings","recipients":[{"session","lifetime_id"}]}]}` |
| `endpoint` | `{"url"}` |
| `sender` | `{"id","version","package":{"name","sha256","base64"}}` |

- `state` is `off`, `starting`, `ready` or `failed closed`. `agent_text` is the state's text for an agent, and
  `operator_text` is the state's text for the operator: two keys, so the gateway drops `operator_text` for a
  caller with no password login. No `agent_text` names a stop or a start (I2).
- `chrome_kind`, `chrome_version` and `chrome_pid` are Chrome's kind, version and pid, and `extension_version` is
  the agent extension's version. Each one is `null` while the service does not have that fact (no Chrome runs).
  `update` is Chrome's update state, as the `update` of the `chrome` event, or `null`: it is the update state of
  the requests' table and of a daemon's connect.
- `endpoint` is the endpoint's state, as in the `ready` event: `{"form":"<form>"}` or
  `{"form":null,"reason":"<text>"}`.
- `mcp` is the MCP's state, for `agentwatch health`: `{"state":"<state>","address":<text or null>}`. `state` is
  `none` while the service serves no MCP (before stage 1d, and from stage 1d while its MCP path is off), and then
  `address` is `null`. From stage 1d `state` is also `ready` while the service serves the MCP with its admission and
  its token check, and `closed` while the service closes each MCP connection with no answer (the Mac, until its peer
  reader exists), each with the MCP's `address` (C11: `127.0.0.1:<port>`); and `failed` when the service could not
  open its MCP path, with the `address` `null`. The daemon shows `address` as it is.
- `sessions` holds each session of the last `set_sessions`, as `{"scope","repo","list","mcp_token"}`: `list` is the
  name of the scope's list, or `null` while the scope has no list, and `mcp_token` is `true` while the session's item
  has a fingerprint, else `false`: `status` never gives the fingerprint. `lists` holds the names of the lists that the
  service has.
- `sign_ins` is the stored sign-ins' state, `{"state","records","acts","pane_key","stopped","unprotected"}` (the stored
  sign-ins, below). A service whose hello has no feature `sign_ins` gives no key `sign_ins`.
- `last_stop` is `null`, or `{"reason","at","by"}`: the reason of the last `stopped` event, its time in UTC
  (ISO 8601, for example `2026-11-10T06:30:00Z`), and its `by` (`null` for a reason other than `stop`).
- `idle_stop_minutes` is the grace time of the stop by use in minutes, as the service applies it (the start and the stop
  by use, below).
- `prompts` (in `status` and in `prompts_get`) holds the open prompts. Each prompt has its `id`, `host` and
  `list`, the list's `repo` (or `null`), the `scopes` that its blocks came from, its `from` host (or `null`), its
  `warnings`, and its `recipients`: the session name (`session`) and the lifetime id (`lifetime_id`) of each
  session that a block of the prompt stopped, from that session's `lifetime_id` in `set_sessions`. The daemon
  sends each recipient the allow notice. No log line holds a lifetime id.
- A recipient's `lifetime_id` is the one of the run that the block stopped: the session's `lifetime_id` in the
  `set_sessions` in force when the block happened, not a later one. A new run of a session comes with a new
  `set_sessions` that carries its new `lifetime_id`. A session whose `lifetime_id` is `null` is still a
  recipient, with `"lifetime_id":null`.
- `lists_get`: each list with its `name`, its `repo` (or `null`), its entries (`allow`, as `list_set` names them),
  the scopes that use it (`sessions`), and its read error (`error`, `""` when it has none); `old_allow` is the
  old single list, read only (`[]` when it cannot be read).
- `endpoint`: `url` is the endpoint's URL for the scope, as a client gives it to Playwright (C11).
- `sender`: `id` is the sender's extension ID, `version` its version, and `package` the sender package (C1): its
  file `name`, its `sha256` (64 hex digits) and its bytes in `base64`.

**The ops of stage 1b part 1 (board ab-v16.94.3).** Stage 1b part 1 adds the Chrome unit to the service (the design,
sections 5.1, 5.6 and 13.2) and the ops `start` and `stop`: the service no longer answers them `unsupported`. Each other
op that stage 1a answers `unsupported` stays so until its stage.

- `start` starts Chrome and Xvfb and answers when they run, with `data` `{}`, or with the start's error text (for
  example a refused switch of `chrome_args`, or that Google Chrome is being installed into the tools folder). `stop`
  stops them and answers when they are gone, with `data` `{}`.
- A `start` or a `stop` while the service does not own the Chrome unit (the design, section 13.2) gets the error
  `the agent-browser service does not own the agent browser now`, and the service changes nothing.
- A `start` and a `stop` answer when their work ends, so their answer can come after the answer of a later request and
  after their events: the daemon matches each answer by its `id`. A daemon that half-closes the connection after a
  request still gets that answer; the service closes the connection after its last answer.
- `ready` comes when Chrome and Xvfb run after a `start`, a restart by the service's supervisor, an adoption at the

  gets a second one: a `start`, a start at a use (board ab-v16.94.11), a restart by the supervisor, the start of a wanted Chrome at the first `config` after
  an adoption that found no live Chrome (or at the service's start, when that `config` came before the adoption
  ended), a move and the way back of a failed move; the daemon follows Chrome's pid (its memory guard) from these
  events.
  Stage 1b part 1 has no native link and no agent package (stage 1b part 2), so
  `extension_version` is `null`, and with no receiver the fail-closed watch stops Chrome 60 s after the last confirm on
  it, with the `stopped` reason `receiver_missing`.
- `chrome` comes when the version or the update state of the running Chrome changes.
- `stopped` comes at a `stop` that stopped a running Chrome (with the stop's `by`), at a stop of the fail-closed watch
  (`receiver_missing` or `sessions_unread`), and once after three failed starts or early exits (an exit within 5
  minutes) in a row (`start_failed`). From board ab-v16.94.11 it also comes at a stop by use (`idle`), and once for a
  run of failed starts at a use (`start_failed`). A `config` with `enabled: false` stops Chrome with no event, also one that came
  before the service's start ended (the adopted Chrome then stops).
- The data of `status` (above) has these values. `state` is `ready` while Chrome and Xvfb run; `starting` during a
  start (a start at a use from its first moment), while the supervisor starts a wanted Chrome again, and while a wanted
  Chrome waits for the first `config`; `failed closed` after a stop of the fail-closed watch; else `off` (also after a
  stop by use: `last_stop` gives its reason `idle`). After a stop of the fail-closed watch, only a `start` or a use
  starts Chrome again: a later `config` starts nothing, and `state` stays `failed closed`.
- `chrome_kind` is `google-chrome` (Google Chrome stable from the tools folder, when `chrome_bin` is empty or `null`)
  or `chromium` (the Chromium of `chrome_bin`): the kinds of the unit record.
- `chrome_version` is the version of the tools folder's copy, in the form of Google's index (`a.b.c.d-r`, for example
  `157.0.7000.1-1`), or `null` when the service does not know it (a Chromium of `chrome_bin`). The `ready` and `chrome`
  events carry the same value.
- `update` is `null` while no Google Chrome of the tools folder runs; else `current`, `pending` (a newer copy waits for
  the next stop by use, and the next start runs it; the move after the idle hour, when no client holds a connection or
  after 24 h, stays the floor), `moving` (a move or the probation of its new copy runs), or `failed` (the last move
  failed, and the last good copy runs). A service from before board ab-v16.94.11 sends `waiting` in place of `pending`.

to the service (the design, sections 5.1, 5.3 and 5.4), and the op `sender`: the service no longer answers it `unsupported`.
Now no op of stages 1a to 1d gets `unsupported` from the service; only the ops of the stored sign-ins do, until stage 1e
part 1 (the stored sign-ins, below). The stage adds no feature name, as the part of stage 1a says.

- `sender` gives the sender of the chrome-extension commit that agent-browser pins (`443f968`, C1). `id` is the sender's ID,

- The agent package. agent-browser pins the chrome-extension commit, the receiver ID (`ifhfjdmdbgbnmllffiadajepajbcdnai`) and

  With a receiver that takes `set_scopes` and no complete `set_sessions`, its reason is `sessions_unread`.

**The connection tokens (from stage 1d, board ab-v16.94.5; the token design, board ab-v16.94.10).** The token design
is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-mcp-connection-tokens-design.md`, with the operator's answers of 2026-11-10.
agentwatch's daemon mints a connection token for each session that it launches while the rules of the token design's
section 9.1 hold (agentwatch launches the session with a lifetime id and the switch on; the session's repo's list has a
site; the service's hello has the feature `mcp_token`; the `LISTEN` line of the MCP path's TCP port has the user's uid;
the client takes the entry's form). It writes the token into the session's MCP entry only after the service took the
token's fingerprint (rule 6). The service never gets a token: `set_sessions`
carries only each token's fingerprint, in the session's own item (`mcp_token`). C11 states the token check of the MCP
path (its admission, step 7).

- No new op and no provenance change: `set_sessions` stays `kind: daemon`.
- The daemon sends `mcp_token` in each item of `sessions`, to every service: the fingerprint of the session's token, or
  `null` for a session that has no token. An item with no `mcp_token` gets `bad request` (the field rules). The daemon
  writes no MCP entry for a service whose hello has no feature `mcp_token`, and it then sends `null` for each session.
- The service keeps the fingerprints of the last `set_sessions` in memory only, never on disk, and it drops a
  session's fingerprint with the session. Its log and its `status` name a scope and say whether the session has a
  token, never a fingerprint.
- The `bad request` answer to `set_sessions` never echoes the snapshot or any field of it: no scope, no lifetime id
  and no fingerprint.
- Before a launch with the entry, the daemon waits up to 5 s for the answer to the `set_sessions` that carries the
  session's fingerprint. At a failure (`bad request`, or no answer in 5 s) it writes no entry file, it launches the
  session without the entry, and its next snapshot carries `"mcp_token":null` for that session.
- When a `set_sessions` that drops a session or a remote fingerprint fails (`bad request`, or no answer in 5 s), the
  daemon sends its current snapshot again after 1 s, then with a delay that doubles up to 60 s, and at each
  reconnect. A `bad request` on such a snapshot is a fault: the daemon's health line is red. "Drops" compares with
  the last snapshot that the service took, so the daemon sends again until the service takes a snapshot without that
  session or that fingerprint. Each resend is the current snapshot, so it also carries each later change. The daemon
  may send every failed snapshot again in the same way, and the service takes each one as it takes any
  `set_sessions`.
- Each item of `remote` carries its remote session's fingerprint in the same field (stage 5: the calling box's daemon
  mints the remote token). `remote` stays empty until stage 5.
- The service's hello gets the feature `mcp_token` in stage 1d, with the MCP path and its token check. The hello
  lists it while `status`'s `mcp` state is `ready`, and only then; `agent-browser service --hello` serves no MCP, so its
  hello has no `mcp_token`. The service opens its MCP path before its control door, so its `mcp` state is fixed before
  its first hello and stays the same for the service's run: each connection of a run gets the same features, and a
  hello with no `mcp_token` means no MCP until the service's next start. The daemon waits for no other line. Before stage 1d
  the service takes the field by the field rules and gives it in `status`, and it serves no MCP.

**The lists, the scopes, the prompts and the sign-ins (stage 1c).** Stage 1c (board ab-v16.94.4) moves this logic
from the daemon into the service (the design, sections 5.1, 7.4 and 7.5). The service talks to the receiver (C4) and
to the model (C10) through one receiver port. In stage 1c the service has no native link: it sends no `set_scopes`
and no `import`, and the model gets no allowlist line, until stage 1b part 2 (board ab-v16.94.9) puts the port on the
native link.

- The files. The lists are today's files in the folder of `--lists-folder`, in today's format: `<list>.json`,
  `{"allow":[<host>...],"repo":"<owner>/<repo>"|null}`. Only the owner of the Chrome unit writes them (the design,
  section 13.2), so the service takes no file lock. The file of ended scopes (`ended-scopes.json`,
  `{"ended":[<scope>...]}`), the list copies and the dismissals are in the folder of `--state-folder`. The service
  replaces each file whole (a new file of mode 0600, then a rename), in a folder of mode 0700.
- A session's repo. A session's `repo` is its GitHub `owner/repo` (the service lowercases it), and the session's
  list is the list of that repo: `<owner>.<repo>`, or `r-` and the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of
  `<owner>/<repo>` when that is no scope name. A `repo` of `null` gives the list `no-repo`. A `repo` string that is
  not an `owner/repo` says that the daemon cannot read the session's repo yet: the scope gets the list
  `unread-repo`, which is always empty, and the service reads no file of that name.
- The object. The service sends `set_scopes` with `default` `null` and `operator` `"*"` (C4). It keeps C4's rules 1
  and 2 of the tabs of a session that ended: no object before the first complete `set_sessions`, the file of ended
  scopes and the restart gate. An ok in time is an ok within 10 s on the same link. Only then does the model get the
  allowlist line, with each session's roots. With no ok in time, the service sends its current object again every
  10 s.
- The end of a session. At `session_ended` the service writes the file of ended scopes first, then sends `end_scope`
  to the model (not for a scope that another live session holds), then sends the object without the scope. Last it
  removes the scope's list copy (not before the first complete `set_sessions`, and not for a scope that another live
  session holds).
- The list copy. The list copy of a scope is `<state>/session-lists/<scope>/allowlist.json`,
  `{"allow":[<host>...]}`: the hosts of the scope's list, for agent-browser's CLI. At each `set_sessions` and each
  list change, the service writes it for each scope that the object names with a list that has a file. At the same
  times it removes each copy that it wrote whose scope the object no longer names (also a launch name that two live
  sessions have: the daemon sends no item for it, by the field rules) or names with no list, `unread-repo` or a list with no file, so the CLI refuses that session and never reads
  an old list's hosts. It never writes or removes a copy through a symbolic link.
- `launch_check`'s refusal is today's text: "A session named <scope> ended a moment ago, and the agent browser could
  not record it (<error>). Choose another name, or wait until the agent browser confirms its lists." The vectors'
  `r6` gives it with the error `the file of ended scopes cannot be written: ENOSPC`, and the fake answers so.
- `list_set`. Each entry of `refused` has the host rules' reason, for example `paste the host only` (the vectors'
  `r7`). A removal that is not valid or not on the list is ignored. The list file's `repo` stays the file's own repo
  when it is the repo of the list's name; else it is the repo of a live session of that list, else `null`.
  `list_set` changes nothing and answers an error for the list `unread-repo` (`the list unread-repo is always empty,
  so it takes no entry`), for a list file that it cannot read (`the list file of <list> cannot be read, so the
  service does not change it`), and for a write that fails (`the list file of <list> cannot be written: <kind>`).
- The lists. `lists_get` holds each list that has a file and each list that a live session uses. The list
  `unread-repo` has the error `the repo of the session's launch folder cannot be read yet; its list is empty until it
  can`. The status's `lists` holds the names of these lists. A session's `list` in the status is the list that the
  current object gives its scope, or `null` while the object gives none (a launch that the restart gate holds, an
  ended name). A launch name that two live sessions have has no session in the status: the daemon sends no item for
  it (the field rules), so neither session gets a list.
- The texts. The service adds sentences to the state's texts. The agent's text gets only "The agent browser's
  extension has not confirmed the lists yet." while a receiver is there and has not confirmed the current object.
  The operator's text gets the errors of the lists and of the file of ended scopes, the receiver's last refusal
  ("The agent extension did not confirm the lists: <error>."), and the count of the open prompts.
- A blocked report. A blocked report (the receiver's `blocked`, or the model's `daemon.blocked`) goes on at most once
  in 10 s for each host, across scopes and sources, and the service keeps at most 256 hosts for this. A report makes a
  prompt only when its scope has a list in the current object, that list does not cover the host, and the operator
  has not dismissed the host for that list. The service keeps one prompt for each host and list, and at most 20 (the
  oldest closes). A prompt's `warnings` are objects `{"kind":"broad"|"shared","text":"<text>"}`.
- The `prompt` event. Its state is `open` for a new prompt, `changed` when the prompt's scopes or its `from` change,
  and `closed` when the prompt closes: at an allow, at a dismissal, when its list covers its host, when one of its
  scopes no longer has its list (also at the end of that scope's session), or at the limit. A `closed` event names the
  prompt's host, its first scope and its list, with `from` `null` and `warnings` empty.
- The dismissals. A dismissal holds 24 hours and outlives a restart of the service: `prompt-dismissals.json` keeps at
  most 500. When the service cannot write a dismissal, `prompt_dismiss` answers the error `the dismissal cannot be
  written: <kind>`, and the prompt stays open. The service keeps open prompts in memory only.
- `import`. It takes the box hosts and cookie names of the last `set_sessions`. Before the first `set_sessions`, or
  with no box host, it answers `the sign-in send is not valid (field box_hosts)`. It drops each cookie whose name is
  one of `box.cookie_names` or starts with `agentwatch` (today's rule), and each cookie that a box host would get. A
  payload of more than 33,554,432 bytes of compact JSON gets `a sign-in send is at most 33554432 bytes`. A C2
  refusal gets `the sign-in send is not valid (field <field>)`: it names the field, never a value. The service runs
  one import at a time, waits at most 45 s for a receiver, and waits 120 s for the receiver's answer. A failed import
  answers the Browser pane's text of the send (today's `send_text`), for example `The agent browser did not answer the
  sign-in for <domain> in time: it can be sent in part or in full.`
- The `error` event. After an `import` whose answer holds errors, the service sends the `error` event: `op` is
  `import`, `user` is the act's user, `domain` is the payload's domain, and `counts` is `{"errors":<their number>}`
  (never their texts, which can name a cookie). The event goes out before the `import`'s answer line, when the
  receiver's answer comes.
- Later answers. An answer can come after the answers of later requests: an `import` answers when the receiver
  answers, and the service answers the other requests of the connection in that time. The daemon matches each answer
  by its `id`. When the daemon ends its side of the connection, it still gets the answers that the service owes it;
  then the service ends its side.
- The log. No log line holds an entry of a `list_set`, a refused entry, a lifetime id, a cookie, a storage value or a
  payload. A log line names a list and its counts, the host of a blocked report, and the domain and the counts of an
  import.

**The stored sign-ins (from stage 1e part 1; board ab-v16.97.2).** The design is
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-stored-sign-in-credentials-design.md` (board ab-v16.95), sections 5, 6 and 8, with
the operator's answers of 2026-11-10 (board item ab-8w8: Q1 a, Q2 b, Q3 b, Q4 a). The service holds the open store key
and no file of the store. The daemon holds the store's files and no open key, and no plain value reaches it. Nothing
uses this part before stage 1e part 1, and no session uses a stored sign-in before stage 3.

- The feature. The service has the eight ops of the stored sign-ins (`sign_ins_setup`, `sign_ins_unlock`,
  `sign_ins_lock`, `sign_ins_rekey`, `sign_in_seal`, `sign_in_reseal`, `sign_in_start_again` and `sign_ins`), the events
  `sign_in` and `sign_ins`, and the key `sign_ins` of `status` only with the feature name `sign_ins` in its hello. A
  service whose hello does not have it answers the eight ops `unsupported`, sends neither event and gives no key
  `sign_ins`. The daemon sends the eight ops only to a service whose hello has it. From stage 1e part 1 the service's
  hello on Linux has the feature `sign_ins`, also the hello of `agent-browser service --hello`. On the Mac its hello has
  no `sign_ins` until stage 4 (C11, the sign-in act).
- Words. A credential is one stored sign-in. The store is the credentials of one OS user on one box: `key.json` and
  one record file for each credential in agentwatch's token store, `tokens/agent-browser/sign-ins/` (0600 files in a
  0700 folder), which only the daemon writes. The store key is an X25519 key pair: its private half is on disk only
  wrapped, with a key that scrypt gives from the passphrase. The pane key is an X25519 key pair that the service makes
  at each start: its private half is only in the service's memory, and its public half is no secret.
- The state. `none`: the store is not open, and the last `sign_ins` has no key, or no `sign_ins` came since the
  service's start. `locked`: the last `sign_ins` has a key, and the service holds no open private half. `open`: the
  service holds the open private half in its memory. Only `sign_ins_setup` and `sign_ins_unlock` open the store.
  `sign_ins_lock`, a `sign_ins` whose `key` is `null` or whose `key.public` is not the open key's public half, and
  each stop of the service drop the open private half. A restart of the daemon, and a stop and a start of Chrome, do
  not drop it.
- The order at each connect: `config`, `set_sessions`, then `sign_ins`, before any other op of the stored sign-ins.
  The daemon sends `sign_ins` again at each change of the store's files.
- The pane's seal (`sealed`). The Browser pane, and the offer command on an agent's side, seal each plain value to the
  pane key's public half with agentwatch's Secret box seal (`screen-seal.js`, `sealPayload`) before the value leaves
  them. `text` is the seal's text (`sealed` in the answer of `sealPayload`: `aw1:` and base64url), `sender` its sender
  key (`senderPublicB64` in that answer: standard base64), `rid` a random request id of 22 to 64 base64url
  characters, and `at` the seal's time in UTC (ISO 8601). The seal's binding has the purpose `browser-sign-in` and the
  fields `op` (the request's op), `rid` and `at`, and for `sign_in_seal` also `id`, `name`, `origin`, `path` and
  `send_to` (from `record`) and `offer`, so a seal for a request with `"offer":true` never opens for a request with
  `"offer":false`, and the reverse, and a seal for one credential's `id` never opens for another `id` (board
  ab-v16.97.4). Each value of the binding is a string: `send_to` is its origins joined with one
  space, `offer` is `"true"` or `"false"`, and `at` is the seal's time in ISO 8601. The sealed object is
  `{"passphrase":"<text>"}` for `sign_ins_setup` and `sign_ins_unlock`, `{"old":"<text>","new":"<text>"}` for
  `sign_ins_rekey`, and `{"username":"<text>","password":"<text>"}` for `sign_in_seal`.
- The binding's bytes. The service builds them as `bindingInfo` of `screen-seal.js` does (agentwatch's
  `mail_crypto.Binding.info`): `agentwatch-browser-sign-in-v1`, one NUL byte, then the fields as JSON with sorted keys
  and no spaces. Each name and value is printable ASCII (the bytes 0x20 to 0x7E): the rules of `record` keep its fields
  to it. The service opens a seal as `mail_crypto.open_payload` does: HPKE (RFC 9180) base mode with DHKEM(X25519,
  HKDF-SHA256), HKDF-SHA256 and ChaCha20-Poly1305, with the binding's bytes as the info and no additional data; then the
  sender tag, HMAC-SHA256 over the info and the JSON, with a key from HKDF-SHA256 of X25519 of the pane key and the
  `sender` key; then the JSON object. The service does not check the JSON's padding: `screen-seal.js` pads it with
  spaces to a multiple of 256 bytes, and `mail_crypto.seal_payload` does not pad.
- The service opens a pane's seal only with the binding that it builds from the request. A seal that does not open with
  that binding, a seal whose `at` is more than 120 s from the service's clock (before or after it), a seal whose `rid`
  the service keeps, a `rid` that is not 22 to 64 base64url characters, an `at` that is not a time in UTC of the form
  `2026-11-10T20:00:00Z` or `2026-11-10T20:00:00.000Z`, and an `at` of that form that is no real time (for example
  `2026-02-30T20:00:00Z`) get `the sealed value cannot be opened`, one text for each case. The service keeps the `rid`
  of each seal whose `rid` and `at` have their form and whose `at` is within 120 s of its clock, until that seal can no
  longer open: until its `at` is more than 120 s in the past and at least 120 s have passed since the service first saw
  the `rid`. The clock of these checks never goes back: it is the highest time that the service's clock gave, so a step
  back of the clock lets no seal whose `rid` the service dropped open again. A new start of the service makes a new pane
  key, so a seal to an older one does not open: the pane gets the new public half from `status` or from the `sign_ins`
  event, and seals again.
- The passphrase. `sign_ins_setup` and `sign_ins_rekey` refuse a new passphrase of fewer than 16 characters with
  `the passphrase has fewer than 16 characters`; `sign_ins_rekey` checks the new passphrase's length before the old
  passphrase. A passphrase that does not open the wrapped key gets `the passphrase does not open the store` 2 s after
  the op's turn began. The service runs one `sign_ins_setup`, `sign_ins_unlock` or `sign_ins_rekey` at a time, in the
  order that they came, and it begins the next one only after that answer, so an op that waits for its turn answers
  later. At most two of them wait behind the one that runs: one more gets `the store is busy; try again` at once, before
  its other checks, with no derivation and no wait. Each op ends within about 3 s (two derivations with N = 2^18, or the
  2 s of a passphrase that does not open the key), so an answer of these ops comes within about 9 s of its request, and
  the daemon waits 15 s for it.
- A lock during a derivation. A `sign_ins_lock` wins over each `sign_ins_setup`, `sign_ins_unlock` or `sign_ins_rekey`
  that came before it and has not answered, also one that waits for its turn or derives its key (the design, section
  5.2: the operator's "Lock" locks the store at once): the op opens nothing. The service reads its count of locks when
  such a request comes. After its derivation each of the three checks the lock and the last `sign_ins` again.
  `sign_ins_setup` answers `the store is set up already` and gives no key when the last `sign_ins` has a key by then;
  else it answers its `key` (the daemon writes it), and the store stays closed when a lock came. `sign_ins_unlock` whose
  passphrase opens the key answers `the store is not set up` when the last `sign_ins` has no key by then, and
  `the store is locked` when a lock came or the key's `public` is not the public half of the key that the passphrase
  opened. `sign_ins_rekey` answers `the store is not set up` or `the store is locked`, by the state, and gives no key,
  when the store is no longer open with the key that it checked.
- The protections. The service opens the store only in a process where both protections of the design's section 5.5
  hold: the process is not dumpable (`prctl(PR_SET_DUMPABLE, 0)` at its start), and the protection of board
  ab-v16.96 holds (hold SIGUSR1 at start; disable the inspector signal). When one does not hold, `sign_ins_setup` and
  `sign_ins_unlock` get `the service's memory is not protected (<reason>)`, with the reason `dumpable` (the call
  failed) or `signal` (the protection of board ab-v16.96 does not hold); `dumpable` comes first when both fail.
  `status` gives the same reason (the status, below).
- `key` is the content of `key.json`:
  `{"v":1,"public":"<base64url>","kdf":{"name":"scrypt","n":<N>,"r":8,"p":1,"salt":"<base64url>"},"wrapped":{"iv":"<base64url>","ct":"<base64url>","tag":"<base64url>"}}`.
  `sign_ins_setup` makes it with N = 131072 (2^17) and a new salt of 16 bytes, and wraps the private half with
  AES-256-GCM under the scrypt key (`maxmem` 257 MiB: scrypt needs 128 times r times (N + 2) bytes and more, so N = 2^18
  does not derive under 256 MiB). The service takes the KDF values of a `key` only within these bounds: `name` `scrypt`,
  N a power of two from 32768 (2^15) to 262144 (2^18), r 8, p 1, and a salt of 16 bytes. Outside them, `sign_ins_unlock`
  and `sign_ins_rekey` get `the store's key file is not valid`, and no derivation runs. A `key` whose `v` is not 1, with
  no string `public`, or with no object `wrapped` of the strings `iv`, `ct` and `tag`, gets the same text. Such a `key`
  still counts as a key: the state is `locked`, and `sign_ins_setup` gets `the store is set up already`.
- The record. A record file is
  `{"v":1,"id","gen","name","origin","path","send_to","repos","by","approved","state","added","changed","sealed"}`. The
  seal binds its plain fields `v`, `id`, `gen`, `name`, `origin`, `path`, `send_to`, `repos`, `by` and `approved` as its
  additional data. With the answer Q2 b, `repos` is `null` in the file, not absent as the design's section 5.3 says: so
  each field of the additional data is in the file, and the service reads the additional data from the file alone.
  `state`, `added` and `changed` are the daemon's, for the Browser pane: they are outside the seal, and no decision of
  the service reads them. `sealed` is `{"epk","iv","ct","tag"}` in base64url: an ephemeral X25519 key; HKDF-SHA256 to a
  32-byte key from two shared secrets, the ephemeral key's with the store's public half and the store's private half's
  with the store's public half (as HPKE's auth mode does, with the store key as the sender and the recipient), so only
  the open store key makes a seal that opens, not a program that reads the `public` of `key.json`; and AES-256-GCM over
  `{"username":<text>,"password":<text>,"totp":null}`.
- The rules of `record`. A `record` that breaks one gets `the stored sign-in is not valid (field <field>)`, which names
  the field, never a value. `id` is `c-` and 8 lowercase hex digits; the daemon makes it. `name` matches C11's scope
  pattern. `origin` is a URL origin as the WHATWG URL parser gives it: `https:`, or `http:` with a loopback host
  (`localhost`, a host that ends in `.localhost`, or `127.0.0.1`), for tests. `path` starts with `/`, holds no `?` and
  no `#`, and holds printable ASCII only (the bytes 0x20 to 0x7E), as the binding needs. `send_to` holds `origin`, and
  only origins by the rule of `origin`, each once.
- Names and ids. A name is unique in the store among the approved records (`approved` `true`): an offer can have the
  name of a credential, and the operator gives it another name at the approval (the design, section 6.3). `sign_in_seal`
  with `kind: operator` and `sign_in_reseal` get `another stored sign-in has this name` when another `id` of the last
  `sign_ins` has that `name` and `approved` `true`. Two approved records of one name in a snapshot each break, so the
  sign-in act's check 2 finds neither (C11). An offer gets its own new id, and an offer never changes or replaces a
  credential (the design, section 6.3): an offer with an `id` that the last `sign_ins` holds, or that has a highest
  `gen` in the service (from a `sign_ins`, or from its own seal also since the last `sign_ins`), gets
  `an offer needs a new id`.
- The order of the checks. After the kind, the service checks a request of the stored sign-ins in this order: the queue
  (`sign_ins_setup`, `sign_ins_unlock` and `sign_ins_rekey`); the rules of `record`; the snapshot; the state; the
  protections (`sign_ins_setup` and `sign_ins_unlock`); the pane's seal; then the op's own checks. The first check that
  fails gives its error. The op's own checks come in this order: for `sign_ins_setup` the new passphrase's length; for
  `sign_ins_unlock` the `key` (its form and its KDF bounds), then the passphrase; for `sign_ins_rekey` the new
  passphrase's length, the `key`, then the old passphrase; for `sign_in_seal` a removed id, an offer's id, then the
  name; for `sign_in_reseal` the id, the origin, whether the record opens, then the name.
- The snapshot and the state. Before the first `sign_ins` since the service's start, `sign_ins_setup`,
  `sign_ins_unlock`, `sign_ins_rekey`, `sign_in_seal`, `sign_in_reseal` and `sign_in_start_again` get
  `the stored sign-ins have no snapshot yet`. `sign_ins_setup` while the last `sign_ins` has a key, or while the store
  is open, gets `the store is set up already`. `sign_ins_unlock` while the store is open answers `{}` and changes
  nothing; while the last `sign_ins` has no key it gets `the store is not set up`. `sign_ins_rekey`, `sign_in_seal`
  and `sign_in_reseal` get `the store is not set up` in the state `none` and `the store is locked` in the state
  `locked`. `sign_ins_rekey` gets `the store is not set up` also while the last `sign_ins` has no key. `sign_ins_lock`
  answers `{}` in each state, and `sign_in_start_again` needs no state.
- The fields that the service seals. The service seals each record with `v` 1, `repos` `null` (the operator's answer
  Q2 b: a credential names no repos), and a `gen` one higher than the highest `gen` that it saw for that `id` (in a
  `sign_ins` or in its own seal), or 1 for an `id` that it never saw. `sign_in_seal` with `kind: operator` seals `by`
  `"operator"` and `approved` `true`. An agent's offer (`sign_in_seal` with `"offer":true`; the operator's answer Q3
  b: an agent that holds a credential may offer it once, for the operator's approval) seals `by` `"offer"`, `approved`
  `false` and `send_to` `[<origin>]`, whatever the request holds. `sign_in_reseal` seals the values of the open record
  again with the fields of `record`, the record's own `by`, and `approved` `true`: a change, or the operator's
  approval of an offer. So only the operator's approval, in the seal, makes an offer usable (C11, the sign-in act,
  check 5). The answer's `record` holds the ten plain fields as the service sealed them, so the daemon writes a record
  that opens.
- The service seals only while the store is open, with the public half that it derives from its open private half,
  never with the `public` of a `key`. It keeps no copy of a value that it opens or seals.
- The op's own checks. `sign_in_seal` with the `id` of a removed credential gets `the stored sign-in was removed`.
  `sign_in_reseal` with an `id` that the last `sign_ins` does not hold gets `there is no stored sign-in with this id`;
  with another `origin` than the record's, `a change of the origin needs a new seal of the password`; and with a
  record that does not open (its tag, its shape, a `gen` lower than the highest that the service saw, or a removed
  id), `the stored sign-in cannot be opened`.
- Start again. `sign_in_start_again` is the operator's "Start again" in the Browser pane (C11, the sign-in act's lockout
  guard). It clears the stop of the credential `credential` and its count of failed results in a row; the times of its
  acts in the last hour stay. It answers `{}`, also for a credential that is not stopped. A `credential` that the last
  `sign_ins` does not hold gets `there is no stored sign-in with this id`. A removed id is no credential: `stopped` in
  `status` never names it, and `sign_in_start_again` with it gets `there is no stored sign-in with this id`, also when a
  later `sign_ins` holds its file again. When an id becomes a removed id, the service drops its stop, its count and the
  times of its acts from its file.
- The snapshot. `sign_ins` replaces the service's last snapshot of the store: its key and its records. A record that
  breaks the record's shape, or that does not open at its use, breaks only itself. Each `id` of the last snapshot that
  a new `sign_ins` does not hold is a removed id from then on. A record that breaks its shape still holds its `id` (a
  string) for this rule, so a file that is bad for a short time removes nothing for good. The answer's `records` is
  the number of records in the request.
- The service's own file. In the folder of `--state-folder`, `sign-ins-state.json` keeps the highest `gen` of each `id`,
  the removed ids, and for each credential the count of its failed results in a row, its stop and the times of its acts
  in the last hour (C11, the sign-in act). It holds ids and numbers only, no secret. A file that the service cannot read
  at its start (for example a damaged one) counts as no file, as on a new box (the design, section 5.6), and the service
  logs the error's kind. When a write fails, the service keeps its state in memory and logs the error's kind.
- The lines that hold a seal. Only the request lines of `sign_ins_setup`, `sign_ins_unlock`, `sign_ins_rekey` and
  `sign_in_seal` hold a pane's seal. Only the request line of `sign_ins` and the answers of `sign_in_seal` and
  `sign_in_reseal` hold a sealed record. Only the request line of `sign_ins` and the answers of `sign_ins_setup` and
  `sign_ins_rekey` hold a wrapped key. No line holds a plain passphrase, username or password.
- The events. `sign_ins` comes at each change of the state, with the state and the pane key's public half. `sign_in`
  comes from the sign-in act's method, `AgentBrowser.signIn` (C11), for each request, also for each call of the MCP
  tool `browser_sign_in` that reaches the method (C11). A call that a remote token admitted never gets past the method's check 1 (C11): it sends check
  1's one refusal `end`, with the result `remote`. `act` is an id of 16 lowercase
  hex digits that the service makes at random for the request. An act sends a `start` (`phase` `start`) and an `end`
  (`phase` `end`) with its `act`. A request that a check before the act refuses (C11, checks 1 to 7) is no act: it
  sends one `end` with its own `act` and no `start`, within the rate of the refusal ends (below). `scope` is the caller's scope, `repo` its session's `repo` in the
  last `set_sessions` (or `null`), `id`, `name` and `origin` are the credential's (`null` for a refusal by check 1 or
  2, which names no credential), and `at` is the time in UTC (ISO 8601). An `end` also has `result` (C11's result),
  `ms` (the time in milliseconds), `blocked` and `guarded` (the number of requests that the act's block and its guard
  stopped), and `stopped_now` (`true` when the act's result stopped the credential: C11's lockout guard). A refusal's
  `end` that the service sends after refusal ends of the same scope that it did not send also has `dropped`, their
  number (the rate of the refusal ends, below).
- The rate of the refusal ends (PR #112's open point 11; the daemon lead's rule). The service sends at most 10 `end`
  events with no `start` (refusal ends) for one scope in any 60 s. It sends no further refusal end of that scope, and
  it counts each one that it does not send. The next refusal end of the scope that it sends after such ones carries
  `dropped`, their number, and it sends that one only when at least 60 s have passed since its last refusal end of the
  scope; the count then starts again. Each scope has its own rate, an act's `start` and `end` are outside it, and each
  refused request still gets its answer and its log line. The vectors' `refusalRate` gives a run of refusals and the
  ends that go.
- No host of a blocked request. No event, prompt or log line names the host of a request that the act's block or its
  guard stopped, because a host that the page chooses can carry the password. A block of the list rules (C4, C5) in
  an act tab or its popups makes no blocked-site prompt, no `prompt` event and no blocked report that names its host:
  the service counts it in the act's `blocked`.
- What the daemon does with them. The live view sends no frame and plays no input from a `start` to the `end` with the
  same `act`, or for 60 s after a `start` with no such `end`. An `end` whose `act` had no `start` ends no blank. An
  `end` with the result `locked` gives the operator one Web Push, at most one in an hour. An `end` with `stopped_now`
  `true` shows the credential as stopped in the Browser pane. The daemon keeps the last 200 `sign_in` events in its own
  state, and the Browser pane shows the last 20 of each credential.
- The status. `sign_ins` in `status` is `{"state","records","acts","pane_key","stopped","unprotected"}`: the state,
  the number of records of the last `sign_ins`, the number of acts that started since the service's start (a refusal
  by a check before the act is no act), the pane key's public half (standard base64), the ids of the stopped
  credentials (a list, empty when none), and the reason of a protection that does not hold (`dumpable` or `signal`:
  the protections, above), or `null` while both hold. It names no name and no origin of a credential.
- The daemon's part. The daemon applies an operator act of the stored sign-ins only with the gateway's field
  (provenance, above), and it logs each one with its user, the op, and the credential's id and name. It takes an
  offer's source from its walk of the offer command's peer pid (`SO_PEERCRED`) to its session, never from a field of
  the request, and it sends the offer with `kind: token`. It checks the service before its hello (who connects,
  above), so only the real service gets a seal.
- The session's repo. The sign-in act takes the caller's scope from the walk, and the scope's list from its session's
  `repo` in the last `set_sessions`. With the answer Q2 b it needs no other field of `set_sessions`, and no `repo_key`
  (the token design, section 11).
- The log. No log line of either side holds a username, a password, a passphrase, a wrapped key (the `wrapped` part
  of `key.json`), a sealed record, a pane's seal, a form value, a page's text, a URL path or a query. The service logs
  each op of the stored sign-ins with its provenance and the credential's id and name, and one line for each request
  of the sign-in act's method (C11).

**The start and the stop by use (board ab-v16.94.11).** The operator answered Q5 on board item ab-1n8 (2026-11-10,
confirmed): "starts/stops should be automatic. when all agents stop using the browser it stops. when at least 1 agent
uses it, it starts. the control plane layer is always active to receive data from any agent at all times. but chrome can
start/stop at will." The service's doors (the agent door, the control door and the MCP path) run at all times and take
each request, also while Chrome does not run; Chrome and Xvfb run only while agents use them.

- The use. Only an agent's real activity, and the operator's send, is a use: nothing of the service's own keeps Chrome
  up. An agent uses the browser while its client connection of the agent door is open (a CDP client that the walk
  admitted: a stop of Chrome would break that client, so it counts by being open), while a client message of such a
  connection runs, while a tool call of an MCP session runs, and while a request waits for the browser (below); the
  operator's send (`import`) is a use while it runs. The time since the last such client message, send or tool call
  counts too, so a use between two checks of the service is not lost. An MCP session counts only by its tool calls,
  while Chrome runs and at step 8 (C11). A tool call that the MCP runs (not a call of one of the service's own tools)
  whose client closed its connection is no use from that close on, also when the MCP runs it on to its answer (C11); a
  call of one of the service's own tools with a local admission stays a use until its act's method ends, also after its
  client's close (C11, the sign-in act). An open MCP session, its other requests (`initialize`,
  `tools/list`, `ping`, a notification, a GET and a DELETE) and the MCP's own connection to the agent door are no use:
  that connection counts neither by being open nor by its messages, because its Playwright sends commands of its own
  (for example when a page attaches a child target). The model's own bridge calls (its content-settings calls at each
  allowlist line, its steps at a link up), the pushes of the lists and their confirms, and a tab's events are no use.
- The start at a use. An upgrade of the agent door, or a tool call of the MCP path, that the readiness refuses because
  the link is down or the allowlist is not confirmed yet (C11), of a scope of a session of the last `set_sessions` while
  `control` is not `false`, goes to the start at a use; another request of the MCP path then passes step 8 with no start
  (C11). First the service makes the checks that it can make while Chrome is down, from its own snapshot (C11): a scope
  that no confirm can name (a scope that two live sessions share), the session check with the roots of the scope's
  sessions in `set_sessions`, and a scope with no list (on the MCP path also a list with no site). Their answer comes at
  once: the request is not held, and it is no use. A live session's scope that the next object leaves out only for the
  restart gate (a launch after the service's start) or for rule 2 (a name that ended while Chrome was off) passes these
  checks: only a push and its confirm, which need Chrome, name it. Only a request that would pass every check but the
  link starts Chrome and Xvfb (one start for all the requests that wait), and it waits until the browser takes it and the
  line names its scope, at most 60 s in all (the wait for a stop by use that runs, the start and the readiness); then its
  checks run again (C11). With no `config` yet, or with
  `enabled: false`, a use starts nothing. After a crash, a use waits for the supervisor's start after its backoff and
  starts no Chrome of its own.
- An `import` (the operator's send) starts Chrome too, before its wait for the receiver, also with `control: false` and
  with no live session: it is the operator's own act on the browser's profile, not an agent's request, and
  `control: false` closes only the agents' doors. With `enabled: false` it starts nothing. When its start fails, the
  import answers at once with the send's text of that error. In the hold-off after a fail-closed stop (below) it starts
  nothing and answers at once with the send's text of that moment, `the agent extension is not connected`.
- `state` is `starting` from the first moment of a start at a use, and while a request waits for the end of a stop by
  use. Each start at a use sends `ready` with its new `chrome_pid`.
- A start at a use that fails. Each request that waits for it gets the 503 `The agent browser could not get ready:
  <reason>.` (C11), where `<reason>` is the start's error text, as `start` answers it. `stopped` with the reason
  `start_failed` goes out once for a run of failed starts at a use, until a start works, and `last_stop` carries it. The
  operator's text gets `The last start at an agent's use failed: <reason>.` For 10 s after a failed start at a use, a
  request gets the same 503 at once, with no new start; a `config` that changed ends that time. A stop that comes during
  the start (the daemon's `stop`, also during the wait for an install, or `enabled: false`) is that stop, not a failed
  start: no `start_failed`, no hold-off, and the request gets that moment's answer.
- The stop by use. When Chrome runs and no agent used the browser for the grace time, the service stops Chrome and Xvfb
  (the owner rule first) and sends `stopped` with the reason `idle` and no `by`; `last_stop` carries it, and `state` is
  `off`. The profile stays on disk, so sign-ins and cookies stay. No stop comes while an agent's client connection is
  open, while a client message, a send or a tool call runs, or while a start, a stop or a move of Chrome runs. A request
  that comes during the stop waits for its end, then starts Chrome again. An open MCP session stays open over a stop by
  use: at its next tool call the request waits for the start at a use, and the MCP's browser connects again with a new
  ticket (C11).
- The grace time. `config` takes it as data from agentwatch's `browser.idle_stop_minutes`, in the key
  `idle_stop_minutes`: a whole number of minutes from 1 to 1440. With no key, with `null` or with another value, the grace
  time is 10 minutes, and the service logs another value. `status` gives the grace time in force (`idle_stop_minutes`).
  The grace time counts from the last use, from each `start` and each start at a use, and for an adopted Chrome from the
  service's start; a start of the service's own (a restart by the supervisor, a move) does not start it again.
- No crash. A stop by use is no fault: the unit record says `wanted: false`, so the supervisor starts nothing after it,
  and the fail-closed watch counts no Chrome.
- After a stop of the fail-closed watch, a use starts Chrome again at most once in 60 s: in that time a request gets that
  moment's answer at once (C11's readiness 503), with no start, and `state` stays `failed closed`. A run of fail-closed
  stops of Chromes that a use started sends its `stopped` once: each later stop of the run sets `last_stop` only, until a
  Chrome stays up past the watch's 60 s.
- The update. A newer Google Chrome that waits shows as `pending` in `update`, in `status` and in the `chrome` event: it
  moves at the next stop by use, and the next start runs it on its probation. The move after the idle hour stays the
  floor.
- `start` and `stop` stay for the daemon's own needs (the switch-off, the uninstall, `stop --all`) and for tests. A use
  after a `stop` starts Chrome again.

**When the daemon drops (a daemon restart or stop).** The service goes on. Chrome, the endpoint and the last
`set_sessions` stay, and the walk refuses each root that is gone. The event stream keeps no event for a later
reader. At each connect the daemon reads `status`, which gives the state, the last stop with its reason and
time, the open prompts and the update state, so the daemon still sees a `stopped` event that it missed. The
next complete `set_sessions` is the truth again.

**The command (stage 1a).** agentwatch's unit runs `agent-browser service --agent-door <address>
--lists-folder <folder> --state-folder <folder> [--control-door <path>] [--daemon-unit <unit>] [--daemon-command
<file>]`. Nothing starts it before stage 2, and no install step of this repository writes a unit for it.

- `--agent-door` (required): the agent door's address, which the daemon gives (today's endpoint path during the
  move: the design, section 13). In stage 1a it is the path of a Unix socket, and the agent door is today's
  endpoint with today's peer check (C11).
- `--lists-folder` (required, from stage 1c): the folder of the lists, `CONFIG_DIR/browser/lists/` (the design,
  section 7.5). The old single list is `allowlist.json` in its parent folder. It has no default, so the service
  never reads the lists of a folder that the unit did not give.
- `--state-folder` (required, from stage 1c): the agent browser's state folder (the design, section 5.2). The
  service keeps `ended-scopes.json`, `session-lists/<scope>/allowlist.json` and `prompt-dismissals.json` in it. It
  has no default.
- `--control-door`: the path of `control.sock`. The default is `control.sock` in the run folder (the door,
  above).
- `--daemon-unit`: agentwatch's unit, whose MainPID rule 1 reads. The default is `agentwatch.service`.
- `--daemon-command`: the file of the daemon's expected command. The default is its path above.
- `--hello` prints the hello and exits (the hello, above). `--fake` serves the fake (below).
- The service runs until `SIGTERM` or `SIGINT`, and then it removes its sockets.

**The command (stage 1b part 1).** `agent-browser service` takes two more flags: `--tools-dir <folder>` (required, with no
default), the tools folder of Google Chrome (today's `~/.local/share/agentwatch-tools/`, which agentwatch's unit gives);
and `--service-unit <unit>`, the service's own unit, whose state decides the owner (the design, section 13.2), by
default `agent-browser.service`. The Chrome unit keeps its files in the folder of `--state-folder` (stage 1c's flag, the
agent browser's state folder): the profile, `unit.json`, the X auth file, `chrome-update.json` and `watch.json`.

- The service owns the Chrome unit only while its own unit is `active`, `activating` or `deactivating` and the unit's
  MainPID is the service. When it does not own at its start, it closes its control door and exits with status 1. When
  it finds later that it no longer owns, it stops its owner parts and exits with status 1. Chrome and Xvfb stay, for
  the next owner.
- `agent-browser service stop --all --state-folder <folder>` stops the Chrome unit with no service running (the
  uninstall, and the Mac's open point 3 of the design's section 17): each Chrome on the profile and the unit's Xvfb, of
  this user only. It finds them also when `<folder>`, or their own arguments, name the folder through a link: it
  compares the real paths. It writes no file.
- `--test` serves the real service for the live harness only: the service owns with no systemd unit, the MainPID of
  rule 1 is the service's parent (the harness), and it needs `--test-display`. It is refused when a folder is missing or
  is a default folder, and when `--test-display` names a reserved display (0, 90, 147, 190, 191 or 192: the harnesses'
  list). Its test-only `--test-chrome-source` names a local folder and a test key in place of Google's
  index. agentwatch never passes these flags.

**The fake service (`--fake`; the daemon lead's C10).** `agent-browser service --fake --control-door <path>`
serves the control door for agentwatch's hermetic tests, with the scripted state of the vectors (the `state` of
`test/vectors/control.json`) and no Chrome. Its code is `src/service/fake.ts`.

- Its hello has `"commit":null` and the features `fake`, `mcp_token` and `sign_ins`. It has every op of the requests'
  table, and it gives each request vector its answer when the vectors run in their order on one connection.
- It takes `mcp_token` by the field rules and keeps it for each session of the last `set_sessions`, in memory only,
  and its `status` gives `mcp_token` (`true` or `false`) for each session. It serves no MCP.
- It admits each connection that reaches its socket: the socket has mode 0600 in a folder of mode 0700, and the
  fake holds no list, no sign-in, no Chrome and no data of the user. It never listens in the service's run
  folder: the folder of the default `control.sock`, and on Linux also `/run/user/<uid>/agent-browser/` (the
  default when `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` is not set). It compares the real folder of its socket (after links, before and
  after it makes the folder) with each of them, so it never takes the place of the service. With `--refuse`, each connection gets the refused
  line of rule 2 of the vectors, and the fake closes it.
- Its own rules where the vectors stop: it starts in the state `ready`. A `start` in the state `off` sends the
  vectors' first `ready` event, and a `stop` in the state `ready` sends `stopped` with the reason `stop` and the
  stop's `by`. An allow or a dismissal closes the prompt, with a `prompt` event in the state `closed`.
  `session_ended` holds the ended scope until the next `set_sessions`. `endpoint` gives the URL of the address of
  `--agent-door` (no agent door listens), else of the vectors' address. Its `config` takes `idle_stop_minutes` by the
  service's rule, and its `status` gives it. It runs no start and no stop by use (no agent door listens): a test sends
  `stopped` with the reason `idle` on its standard input, and then its `state` is `off` and its `last_stop` carries the
  reason `idle` with no `by`, as the service's.
- Its stored sign-ins (the feature `sign_ins`; the vectors' `state.sign_ins`). The fake opens no seal, makes no key and
  seals nothing, so it checks no binding and no seal's time on its own clock. It follows the rules of the state, of the
  errors, of the names and ids and of the fields that the service seals (the stored sign-ins, above), and the rules of
  the form of `rid` and `at`, of an `at` that is no real time and of a `rid` that it keeps (until the seal's `at` is
  more than 120 s in the past by the fake's clock and at least 120 s have passed since it first saw the `rid`).
  `sign_ins_setup` answers the vectors' `key`, and `sign_ins_rekey` their `rekeyed`. `sign_ins_unlock` opens the store,
  except with a seal whose `text` is the vectors' `wrong_passphrase`, which gets
  `the passphrase does not open the store` 2 s after the op's turn began, as the service answers it. The fake keeps the
  service's queue: one `sign_ins_setup`, `sign_ins_unlock` or `sign_ins_rekey` at a time, at most two that wait, and
  `the store is busy; try again` at once for one more. A seal whose `text` is the vectors' `short_passphrase` or
  `short_rekey` stands for a new passphrase of fewer than 16 characters (`short_rekey` in `sign_ins_rekey`, with an old
  passphrase that does not open the store), and a seal whose `text` is the vectors' `stale_seal` stands for a seal more
  than 120 s from the service's clock: the fake takes no `rid` of it, as the service takes none of a seal that its time
  refuses. `sign_in_seal` and `sign_in_reseal` answer the fields that the service seals and the vectors' `sealed`. A
  record of a `sign_ins` does not open in the fake only for its `gen` (lower than the highest, or not a whole number of
  1 or more) or its removed `id`. The fake runs no act, so its `status` gives `acts` 0 and `stopped` `[]`. With
  `--unprotected dumpable` or `--unprotected signal`, it answers as a process where that protection does not hold (the
  vectors' `unprotected`). Each change of the state sends a `sign_ins` event with the vectors' `pane_key`.
- Each line on its standard input that is an event of the event table, with exactly its fields, goes to each
  connection that has sent its hello. The fake drops each other line. So a test sends the events that it needs.
- After its socket listens, the fake prints one line on its standard output, `{"t":"listening"}`, so that a test
  waits for that line.

**The vectors.** `test/vectors/control.json` holds lines of each kind: the two hellos, a `config`, a `set_sessions`,
operator acts with their provenance, a start with the token, their answers, events, a refused connection, and lines that
break a rule, each with the answer that the service gives. Its `state` is the service's state for these lines and the
fake's state at its start; the reads (`q1` to `q5`) give the keys of the data of the reads. Its `badLines` are lines as
bytes (in hex) that close the connection: a byte order mark, a CR, and bytes that are not UTF-8. Its `set_sessions`
lines carry `mcp_token`, and its bad lines `b22` to `b32` break each rule of `mcp_token`, each with the fixed
`bad request` answer. `r14` is the daemon's snapshot while two live sessions have one launch name: it has no item for
that name. `r15` is its next snapshot after one of them ended, with no `session_ended`: it names the survivor with its
own `lifetime_id` and `"mcp_token":null`. Its `testTokens` are made-up test values in a token's form, with their
fingerprints: the door never carries a token. Its `state.sign_ins` holds the fake values of the stored sign-ins, its
`unprotected` the cases of a protection that does not hold (each on a new fake with `--unprotected` and the case's
`reason`; in the case where both protections fail the `reason` is `dumpable`, which comes first), with seals that the
service refuses for their form, so the protections come before the pane's seal, and its `signInScan` the canaries of the
scan: no line of the vectors holds a canary in one of the five forms of the sign-in act's guard (C11), and only the
lines that the stored sign-ins name hold a seal. Each pane's seal of its lines is a seal that agentwatch's
`screen-seal.js` (`sealPayload`) made with fixed test keys, to the test pane key of `paneSeal`, whose private half is a
test value; `paneSeal` also holds one seal with its binding, the binding's bytes, its test keys and its payload, for the
tests of both sides. The real service gives each line of the stored sign-ins its answer too, except the values that it
makes from new random bytes (the `key` of `sign_ins_setup` and `sign_ins_rekey`, and the `sealed` of `sign_in_seal` and
`sign_in_reseal`): the daemon writes the service's own answers, so a test sends them in the later `sign_ins` in place of
the vectors' `key`, `rekeyed` and `sealed`. Its `busy` holds the case of a full queue, on a new fake or service: its
`first` lines run in their order, each after its answer; then its `pipelined` lines go in one write, and `order` gives
their ids in the order of their answers (the last line gets `the store is busy; try again` first). From board
ab-v16.94.11 its `config` line `r1` carries `idle_stop_minutes`, its `status` answer gives `idle_stop_minutes`, and its
events hold a `stopped` with the reason `idle` and a `chrome` event with the `update` `pending`.
